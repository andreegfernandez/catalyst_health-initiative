Portfolio 01 / Health outcomes

Statin prescribing &
cardiovascular mortality.

What is the relationship between statin prescribing and ischaemic heart disease mortality across regions in Sweden?

The question

Looking beyond a national average.

The study examined whether differences in regional statin prescribing intensity were associated with ischaemic heart disease mortality over time.

Using regional data across nine years, the team explored the relationship at a population level, accounting for differences between regions and calendar years.

21Swedish regions
2016–2024Study period
189Region-year observations

The approach

An ecological panel study.

The analysis combined observations from all 21 Swedish regions between 2016 and 2024. The primary model used region and year fixed effects to assess the association between prescribing intensity and mortality while accounting for regional differences and common changes over time.

A mixed-effects model provided a sensitivity analysis.

What the study found

The primary model did not find a statistically significant association between regional statin prescribing intensity and ischaemic heart disease mortality.

The reported coefficient was −0.213, with a 95% confidence interval from −0.556 to 0.130 (p = 0.223). The mixed-effects sensitivity analysis was consistent with the primary finding.

Interpreting the finding

This was an observational analysis of regions, not individual patients. It does not establish causation or determine the effectiveness of statins for an individual.

The contribution

A clearer view of what regional data can tell us.

The report provides a structured examination of prescribing and mortality across Sweden, with an explicit method and a transparent account of the result.
